L2395@P67: A work breakdown structure (WBS) deconstructs a program’s end product
L2396@P67: into smaller specific elements that are suitable for management control.
L2397@P67: The WBS is the cornerstone of every program because it defines in detail
L2398@P67: the work necessary to accomplish a program’s objectives. The WBS
L2399@P67: provides a consistent framework for planning and assigning responsibility
L2400@P67: for the work, and is an essential element for identifying activities in a
L2401@P67: program’s integrated master schedule. The WBS is initially set up when a
L2402@P67: program is established and becomes successively detailed over time as
L2403@P67: more information becomes known about the program. Establishing a
L2404@P67: product-oriented WBS is a best practice because it allows a program to
L2405@P67: track cost and schedule by defined deliverables, such as a hardware or
L2406@P67: software component.
L2407@P67: A WBS provides a basic framework for a variety of related activities
L2408@P67: including estimating costs, developing schedules, identifying resources,
L2409@P67: and determining where risks may occur. It also provides the framework to
L2410@P67: develop a schedule and cost plan that can easily track technical
L2411@P67: accomplishments—in terms of resources spent in relation to the plan, as
L2412@P67: well as completion of activities—enabling quick identification of cost and
L2413@P67: schedule variances.
L2414@P67: A WBS diagrams effort in small discrete pieces, or elements, to show how
L2415@P67: each element relates to the others and to the program as a whole. It can
L2416@P67: be thought of as an illustration of what work will be accomplished to
L2417@P67: satisfy a program’s requirements. Elements such as hardware, software,
L2418@P67: and data are further broken down into specific lower-level elements. The
L2419@P67: lowest level of the WBS is defined as the work package level. By breaking
L2420@P67: work down into smaller elements, management can more easily plan and
L2421@P67: schedule the program’s activities and assign responsibility for the work.
L2422@P67: A WBS breaks down product-oriented elements into a hierarchical parentchild structure that shows how elements relate to one another as well as
L2423@P67: to the overall end product. A well-defined WBS clearly delineates the
L2424@P67: logical relationship of all program elements and helps promote
L2425@P67: accountability by identifying work products that are independent of one
L2426@P67: another. Failing to include all work for all deliverables can lead to
L2427@P67: schedule delays and subsequent cost increases. It can also result in
L2428@P67: confusion among team members.
L2429@P67: A WBS is an essential part of developing a program’s cost estimate and
L2430@P67: enhancing an agency’s ability to collect data necessary to support future
L2431@P67: cost estimates. A WBS also facilitates establishing the schedule, cost,
L2432@P67: and earned value management (EVM) baseline. Its hierarchical nature
