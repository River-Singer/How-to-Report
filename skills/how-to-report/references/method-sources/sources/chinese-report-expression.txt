Original synthesis: Choose sentence structure by communicative purpose. Use a concrete business object and its state/change/comparison for a finding. Use a verb with an explicit object for a proposed action. Use noun phrases for dimensions, columns, indicators and process labels, and restore concrete actions when explaining implementation. Omit a repeated subject when it is recoverable, and state the actor or population when responsibility, contribution or comparison changes. An analytical paragraph links a meaningful finding to supporting comparisons and business implications. A proposal links an action to a relevant audience, situation and expected observation. Necessary scope, period and proposal/prediction identity remain attached to the meaning. These are original cross-source editing choices rather than a proprietary writing standard or evidence that a phrase is human-authored.
